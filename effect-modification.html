Confounding vs. effect modification

Both are revealed by stratifying on a third factor, but they mean opposite things: one is a bias to remove, the other a finding to report.

Diagnose the study

Each card shows results from a stratified analysis. Is it confounding, effect modification, both, or neither?

What to notice

  • Confounding needs the third factor to be linked to BOTH the exposure and the outcome. Set the smoking–exposure link to 0, or the risk multiplier to 1, and the crude RR equals the adjusted RR.
  • Effect modification is about the stratum-specific RRs differing from each other. It does not need any link between smoking and the exposure, and randomization does not remove it.
  • When effect modification is present, a single adjusted (pooled) RR averages over strata whose effects truly differ, so report the stratum-specific RRs.
  • Effect modification depends on the scale: effects that differ on the ratio scale (RR) may not differ on the difference scale (risk difference).
