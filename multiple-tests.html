Sequential vs. simultaneous testing

Two tests can be combined in two ways: retest only the people who were positive the first time, or give both tests to everyone and call anyone positive on either test positive. Drag the sliders and follow 1,000 people through each strategy.

What to notice

  • Sequential: net sensitivity = sensitivity A × sensitivity B, so sensitivity is lost, but specificity is gained. Fewer false positives, and fewer second tests to pay for.
  • Simultaneous: net specificity = specificity A × specificity B, so specificity is lost, but sensitivity is gained. Fewer missed cases.
  • The choice also depends on practical matters: how severe the disease is, cost, invasiveness of each test, length of hospital stay and insurance coverage.
  • These formulas assume the two tests make errors independently of each other.
