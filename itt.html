How each analysis groups patients

Intention-to-treat compares patients by the group they were randomized to, whatever they actually received. It preserves randomization, so the groups stay comparable; the estimate is usually diluted towards 1 (more conservative).

As-treated compares patients by the treatment they actually received. People who switch are rarely a random subset, so this breaks randomization and can bring back confounding.

What to notice

  • Load “Frail patients refuse Drug A”. The drug does nothing (intention-to-treat ratio = 1.00), yet the as-treated analysis makes it look protective, because sicker patients crossed over to current treatment.
  • Set every crossover cell to 0: with full compliance the two analyses agree.
  • Crossovers move the intention-to-treat estimate towards 1. That is the price of keeping randomization intact.
