Type I and type II errors

A trial compares therapy A with therapy B. The grey curve shows the results we would see if the therapies truly do not differ; the blue curve, if they truly do. Drag the decision line, the effect size and the sample size to see how the two kinds of error trade off.

Possible trial results

Type I error, α (false positive) Type II error, β (false negative) Power, 1 − β

Possible conclusions

In reality: treatments do not differIn reality: treatments differ
We conclude: no difference(a) Correct(b) Type II error
We conclude: they differ(c) Type I error(d) Correct: power

What to notice

  • Drag the decision line right (smaller α): fewer false positives, but more false negatives. With a fixed design you cannot shrink both errors at once.
  • Increasing the sample size narrows the overlap between the curves, so power rises without changing α. This is why trials calculate sample size in advance.
  • A small true effect needs a large trial. A “non-significant” result from a small trial is weak evidence that the treatments do not differ.
