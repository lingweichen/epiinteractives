What to notice

  • Concealment protects the randomization sequence during recruitment, before assignment, and prevents selection bias.
  • Blinding works after randomization, keeping participants, investigators or assessors unaware of the treatment received, and prevents information (observation or ascertainment) bias.
  • Blinding matters most when the outcome is subjective, such as self-reported pain. A placebo makes blinding possible, but a placebo or sham is not always feasible or ethical.
