What to notice

  • With early treatment switched off, the screened patient “survives” longer after diagnosis and passes the 5-year mark, yet dies on the same day. Screening added disease time, not life.
  • Screening is truly effective only if survival of the screened is longer than lead time plus the survival of the unscreened, which means death is actually delayed. Compare mortality, not survival from diagnosis.
  • Over-diagnosis adds people who were never going to die of the disease, inflating survival among those diagnosed while deaths stay the same.
  • A screening round preferentially picks up slow-growing tumours, which have a better prognosis anyway (length-time bias). A randomized trial counting all outcomes in both arms avoids both biases.
