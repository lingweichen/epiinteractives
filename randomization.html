Does trial size matter?

Randomize 1,000 trials of each size. Bars show the average gap between arms in the share of patients with the unmeasured risk factor (40% of patients have it).

What to notice

  • Hand allocation can balance what you can see, but you can only match on variables you know about and can measure. Randomization also balances unmeasured confounders.
  • Randomization tends to balance both measured and unmeasured factors, but it is not a guarantee, especially in small trials.
  • Stratified randomization makes sure the arms are balanced on the factors you stratify on (here sex and age group); the unmeasured factor is still left to chance.
  • With more participants, chance imbalances shrink.
