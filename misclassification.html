What to notice

  • Non-differential: exposure is misclassified equally in cases and controls (for example, a common pesticide left off the questionnaire). With a binary exposure, the odds ratio is pulled towards the null (1.0), so estimates are usually more conservative.
  • Differential: misclassification is related to disease status, for example recall bias (cases search their memory harder) or interviewer bias. The bias can go in either direction, away from or towards the null.
  • Lowering specificity in cases only (cases over-report use) reproduces the office hour example: the odds ratio rises from 1.29 to 1.36.
